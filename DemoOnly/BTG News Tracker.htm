Summary for Singapore AI

Summary:
Singapore has launched a coordinated national AI push led by Prime Minister Lawrence Wong, targeting advanced manufacturing, connectivity, finance and healthcare and establishing a National AI Impact Programme. The government is rolling out multiple initiatives including a Champions of AI programme, a new training academy for public officers, and targets to train 100,000 AI-savvy workers and equip 10,000 enterprises by 2029, while offering free premium AI subscriptions for certain SkillsFuture courses from the second half of 2026. Policy measures to attract talent include a new ONE Pass (AI and Tech) visa track with a S$30,000 income requirement, and Singapore and South Korea have launched a Korea–Singapore AI Alliance backed by a US$300 million fund to be based in Singapore. The rollout is accompanied by education and social measures — the Ministry of Education and ministers stress preserving critical thinking in students as GEP is phased out — even as workers brace for disruption and many employees are already adopting AI tools (69% use AI to improve work quality), often not those provided by employers.

Notable signals/trends:
- A whole-of-government, industry and skills approach: coordinated national strategy, public-sector training academy, Champions of AI and the National AI Impact Programme to mainstream AI across organisations and sectors.
- Internationalisation and talent focus: bilateral AI partnership with South Korea with a US$300m fund and a revamped high-income ONE Pass to attract global AI and tech talent.
- Workforce and education tension: aggressive upskilling targets and free tools juxtaposed with concerns about entry-level job displacement, widespread employee use of non-company AI tools, and emphasis on retaining critical thinking in schools.
